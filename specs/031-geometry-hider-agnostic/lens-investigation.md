# The raytracer's depth-of-field noise: investigation (FR-025, US7 AS3; Phase 10, T130 and T131)

**Status**: investigation only. No `src/` file was edited for it. **Decision (PAUSE 10, 2026-11-12)**: no proposal is implemented; the code, the scenes, the filter default and the references stay as they are. The finding is filed as GitHub #166 ("Depth-of-field noise: the default Catmull-Rom pixel filter adds noise and makes it non-monotone in PixelSamples") with P1, P2 and P5 as future options.
**Tree**: Phase 9 (`3c2a81c0`), macOS, `build` (Release). **Scripts and raw output**: `tools/lens-investigation/` (`p10lib.py` and `e*.py` with their `.out`, `slots/dump.cpp`); they write under the worktree's `build-logs/p10/` and need no tracked scene.

## 0. What was measured, and how

Method (research R10, the visual-test noise method): render a scene twice with different seeds (`Option "sampling" "seed" [0|1]`, the only difference), and measure the run-to-run difference. The metric is the RMSE over all four channels of the 8-bit RGBA output (0..255), plus the 99.9th percentile of the absolute difference, the share of values off by more than 64 (isolated bright or dark samples) and the 8x8 block-average maximum the visual tests use. The "error" is the RMSE against a converged render (the raytracer at 12x12 or 16x16 samples per pixel, same scene). The sampler is deterministic, so a pair is exactly reproducible and four pairs (`seed` 0..7) spread by at most 0.3 RMSE.

Scenes: the tracked `camera-dof-raytrace` and `parity/dof-raytrace` (and their Reyes twins) at full size (800x600) for the headline numbers; for sweeps the same scene at 400x300 (the blur radius in pixels is half), generated by `p10lib.py scene()`; and scratch scenes (not tracked, in `e5.py` and `e5b.py`): a small bright defocused sphere on black, a glossy sphere with a tight highlight at z = 12 (focus 5), and a defocused cone at z = 2.5 over a sharp plane at the focal distance with in-focus black dots (US7 AS4). The default settings throughout: `DepthOfField 2 1 5`, `PixelSamples 3 3`, the default pixel filter.

Blur radius at `DepthOfField 2 1 5`, from the lens model (Phase 9): `|c| = imagePlane * aperture * |1/f - 1/z|`, 9 pixels at z = 10 at 400x300 (18 at 800x600).

## 1. Hypothesis 1: shading per lens sample (the raytracer shades every ray hit, Reyes shades once)

- **The tracked scenes: not the cause.** Their surfaces are matte or flat. Noise as a share of the scene's contrast (own ground truth per surface), raytrace against Reyes: default surface 0.272 / 0.216; `constant` (no shading at all) 0.275 / 0.254; lit `matte` 0.143 / 0.246; lit `plastic` 0.143 / 0.169 (`e1b.out`). With the shading removed the raytracer is as noisy as with it, and in the lit scenes it is *less* noisy than Reyes (why Reyes is noisier in those lit scenes was not investigated).
- **A defocused tight specular highlight: the cause, and it is physical.** Scratch scene "tight highlight" (plastic, Ks 4, roughness 0.02, a point light; the sphere at z = 12): raytrace against Reyes noise RMSE 4.59 / 2.42 at 3x3 samples and 1.76 / 0.99 at 6x6; share of values off by more than 64: 0.0010 / 0.0001 (`e5b.out`). The image shows it: the raytracer's highlight is a scatter of isolated bright samples, Reyes's is a smooth glow. The raytracer is also the one that matches the converged render (error 1.29 against Reyes's 2.41 at 6x6): the highlight of a sphere moves with the lens point, so each lens sample shades a different place, which a real lens does too, and Reyes, shading once, cannot. So these isolated bright samples are the converging estimate of a real effect; the only ways to remove them are more samples (stop-and-ask: `PixelSamples`), filtering them (section 4) or coupling shading to visibility differently in the raytracer (stop-and-ask).
- Not a general speckle source: with a lit matte scene, the diffuse look of the tracked scenes, the raytracer is not noisier than Reyes.

## 2. Hypothesis 2: too few lens samples for the blur area

- **Noise against blur size** (400x300, 9 samples per pixel, default filter): raytrace noise RMSE 3.9, 5.6, 9.0, 15.3, 27.0 for f-stop 16, 8, 4, 2, 1 (blur radius at z = 10: 1.1, 2.3, 4.5, 9.1, 18.1 px); Reyes 3.3, 4.6, 7.0, 12.1, 19.2 (`e1.out`). Both grow with the blur, the raytracer 19 to 41 percent higher: 9 samples per pixel spread over an area of tens to hundreds of pixels cannot reconstruct a smooth bokeh.
- **Noise against samples per pixel**, default filter, mean of four pairs (`e2c.out`): raytrace 13.64, 15.29, 11.34, 11.52, 9.76, 9.72, 8.76 for 2x2 to 8x8 samples (4, 9, 16, 25, 36, 49, 64 per pixel), Reyes 14.27, 12.19, 9.07, 8.32, 7.05, 6.59, 5.81. The convergence is slower than `1/sqrt(N)` (64 against 9 samples should give 0.38, the raytracer gives 0.57, Reyes 0.48), and **the raytracer is not monotone**: 9 samples per pixel is noisier than 4, and 49 is no better than 36. Reyes is monotone. Section 4 shows what causes the bump.
- **Stratification is already in place**: the same scenes with `Option "sampling" "sampler" "independent"` are 22 to 26 percent noisier than the default Sobol at both 3x3 and 4x4 (raytrace 18.92 against 15.29 and 13.82 against 11.34; Reyes 15.02 against 12.19 and 11.47 against 9.07; `e2d.out`), and PMJ02 is within 2 percent of Sobol. The gain from the stratified camera slots is the same at a power of two as at nine samples, so the stratification of the lens slot is not what limits the raytracer.

## 3. Hypothesis 3: correlation between the film and the lens dimensions

`slots/dump.cpp` draws the camera slots exactly as the hiders do (`cameraDraws`) for 64x64 pixels (`e3.out`):

| samples per pixel | backend | film cells of nxn occupied | lens cells occupied | film.u x lens.u cells | minimum lens distance | corr(film.u, lens.u) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3x3 (9) | Sobol | 6.88 of 9 | 7.09 of 9 | 6.67 | 0.178 | -0.013 |
| 3x3 | PMJ02 | 6.94 | 6.94 | 6.51 | 0.163 | +0.005 |
| 3x3 | independent | 5.89 | 5.89 | 5.87 | 0.085 | +0.004 |
| 4x4 (16) | Sobol | 16.00 of 16 | 16.00 | 11.41 | 0.136 | -0.001 |
| 4x4 | independent | 10.29 | 10.32 | 10.28 | 0.046 | +0.004 |

There is **no linear correlation** between the film and lens slots (|r| < 0.013), and the joint film x lens occupancy is at or above the random level. One structural fact: a pixel's block of the Sobol sequence is 2^k long and nx*ny samples are used, so at 3x3 the 9 samples cover 7.1 of 9 lens cells where 4x4 covers all 16. That gap is real but small in effect (section 2: the gain over independent sampling is the same at 3x3 and 4x4). The 2D noise spectrum of the sampler output was **not** computed; the per-pixel stratification and the correlation above are what was measured for this hypothesis.

## 4. Hypothesis 4: the pixel filter relative to the blur (the main finding)

**The default pixel filter is Catmull-Rom of width 2** (`options.cpp:285-287`). It has negative lobes. Noise RMSE at 9 samples per pixel with the filter changed, both hiders (`e1.out`, and the tracked scenes at full size, `e6.out`):

| scene, 3x3 | default (Catmull-Rom 2) | Gaussian 2 2 | Gaussian 3 3 | box 1 1 | Catmull-Rom 4 4 | sinc 4 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `camera-dof-raytrace` 800x600 | 15.06 | **10.41** | 9.73 | | | |
| `camera-dof-reyes` 800x600 | 12.03 | **9.73** | 7.83 | | | |
| sweep scene 400x300, raytrace | 15.28 | 10.32 | 9.51 | 14.15 | 16.53 | 16.60 |
| sweep scene 400x300, Reyes | 12.13 | 9.72 | 8.02 | 12.97 | 15.26 | 15.26 |

- A Gaussian filter of the **same width** as the default removes 31 percent of the raytracer's noise (15.06 to 10.41) and 19 percent of Reyes's (12.03 to 9.73); wider Catmull-Rom and sinc filters are worse, because their negative lobes weigh an isolated bright (or dark) sample against its neighbours.
- The noise-against-samples bump of section 2 is a Catmull-Rom effect: with a box or a Gaussian filter the raytracer is monotone in the number of samples (box 17.75, 14.10, 11.68, 10.58, 9.60 and Gaussian 2 2 11.03, 10.41, 8.55, 8.38, 7.55 for 2x2 to 6x6, `e2e.out`); with Catmull-Rom 2 it is 13.59, 15.25, 11.29, 11.53, 9.72.
- The worst isolated values also fall: the 99.9th percentile of the difference 219 to 170 (raytrace), the share off by more than 64 from 0.0089 to 0.0038. The 8x8 block-average maximum the visual tests use does **not** improve (39.6 to 37.4): it is dominated by the defocused silhouettes' coverage noise, which no filter of this width removes.
- **The cost**: a Gaussian is softer. A 2-pixel black dot in focus (converged render, `e7.out`) drops to a minimum value of 168 with Gaussian 2 2 (174 with 3 3) from 133 with Catmull-Rom 2: the default keeps 28 percent more contrast on the finest in-focus detail. The filter is a global setting, not a depth-of-field one.

## 5. US7 AS4: foreground, background and highlights (scratch scenes, viewed)

Defocused cone at z = 2.5 over a sharp plane at the focal distance: in both hiders the cone's edge blends over the sharp plane, and the in-focus dots stay sharp; raytrace and Reyes noise 8.04 and 6.54 (`e5.out`), the shapes look alike in the two hiders (viewed, not measured against the converged render). The bright small defocused sphere (constant surface): both hiders draw the same disk, noise 8.18 and 8.88, the converged error 7.52 and 11.89. The glossy sphere: section 1. Nothing measured here argues against the Phase 9 lens: the mirrored near field and the disk shape are what a thin lens gives.

## 6. The cause, summarised (FR-025)

1. **The isolated bright samples** are real light: a defocused specular highlight is shaded at a different place for each lens point; Reyes shades once and cannot show it. Only the raytracer reproduces it, and only a highlight triggers it (section 1).
2. **The speckle of the tracked scenes** (matte, flat) is coverage noise of 9 samples per pixel over a blur tens of pixels wide (section 2), amplified by the default Catmull-Rom filter (section 4: 31 percent for the raytracer, 19 percent for Reyes), which also makes the raytracer's noise non-monotone in the sample count. It is a reconstruction-filter effect that the same scenes show in both hiders.
3. **The sampler and the lens mapping are not at fault**: no film-lens correlation, stratification already worth 22 to 26 percent over independent sampling at every count, concentric map area-preserving (sections 2 and 3).

## 7. Proposed changes (none is made; each is for the maintainer's decision)

Every proposal below leaves the blur diameter untouched: the filter only reconstructs, and `Camera_LensCircleOfConfusion` and `Camera_LensRayMatchesCoC` (the analytic proof) are not affected by it.

| # | Proposal | Measured effect | Cost and boundary |
|---|---|---|---|
| P1 | Use a Gaussian pixel filter in the depth-of-field scenes (a scene-level `PixelFilter "gaussian" 2 2` in the DoF RIBs, or a documented recommendation), no code change | raytrace noise -31 percent, Reyes -19 percent at 3x3; removes the sample-count bump | the tracked DoF scenes and their references would change (each through the reference approval of T124 again) and in-focus detail is softer (section 4); the default stays Catmull-Rom |
| P2 | Change the **default** pixel filter (a global decision, every image changes) | same noise gain everywhere | **stop-and-ask** and outside this spec's "only depth-of-field images change"; reported, not proposed |
| P3 | Lens-point mapping: another disk map or a finer stratification of the existing slot | at most the 7 of 9 against 16 of 16 cell gap of section 3, which the measurements of section 2 do not show costs anything; no gain is expected | allowed within the lens slot (FR-025, FR-030); no experiment justified by the evidence, so none proposed |
| P4 | A per-sample radiance clamp or a firefly filter in the raytracer | would remove the highlight speckle | biased (changes the highlight's energy), so it would change the physically correct image; not proposed |
| P5 | More samples (the `PixelSamples` default), adaptive sampling, or shading decoupled from visibility in the raytracer | these are the real cures for sections 1 and 2 | **stop-and-ask** by the spec; reported only |

Recommendation: P1 as a scene-level choice if the maintainer wants a quieter raytracer bokeh now; otherwise record the finding and leave the code as it is. The maintainer decided at PAUSE 10 to make no change (GitHub #166 tracks the filter finding and P1, P2 and P5 as future options).

## 8. Limits of this investigation

- The sweeps run at 400x300 (the tracked scenes at 800x600 give the same ordering, section 4); image statistics are from a small scratch tool (`p10lib.py`), not the visual tests' own comparison, so the block-average numbers are given only where they matter.
- The noise spectrum (hypothesis 3) was not computed, only the stratification and the correlation.
- One highlight scene (a distant point light, a sphere at z = 12): the speckle's size depends on the roughness and the blur; it was not mapped over those.
- All measurements are macOS; the Linux run is not needed for an investigation with no change.
